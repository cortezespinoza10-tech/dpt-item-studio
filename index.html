E. Pre-item setup: narrow the AI to your sources

Optional

Items are only as accurate as the material the AI draws from. Upload your own course materials first, so questions, answers, and rationales come from your lectures, the CPGs, and your assigned textbooks instead of the open internet.

Set up a notebook in Google NotebookLM or Gemini

  1. Go to notebooklm.google.com (or gemini.google.com) and sign in with your university Google account, not a personal Gmail.
  2. Create a new notebook named for the course and exam, for example DPT 641 Exam 2 – Lumbar spine.
  3. Add sources: lecture slides and notes (PDF or Google Slides), the CPG PDFs, assigned textbook chapters, and your syllabus objectives.
  4. Check that every source finished loading and is selected before you paste the starter prompt.

NotebookLM answers from the sources you add and links each answer to the passage it came from, which makes it the better fit for exam writing. In Gemini, attach the same files to the chat or to a Gem.

What did you upload?

Your answers are written into the starter prompt so the AI knows what to draw from.

Use your university Google account to keep course materials protected

When you sign in with your university Google Workspace account and the university has Gemini and NotebookLM turned on, Google states that your chats, uploaded files, and responses are not reviewed by humans and are not used to train AI models. They stay inside the university's Workspace under the controls IT already manages, such as data loss prevention policies. A personal Gmail account does not carry these education terms.

  • Confirm the account: the profile picture in the top-right corner should show your university email before you upload anything. If it shows a personal address, switch accounts.
  • Still keep out: student names, grades, accommodations, or any FERPA-protected records. These protections reduce risk; they do not make an AI tool a place for student records.
  • Copyright: upload only materials you are licensed to use, and follow your library's terms for e-textbooks. Many CPGs are freely available from the academies and journals.
  • Not sure it is enabled? Ask your university IT or Center for Teaching which AI tools are approved for faculty.

Format for Canvas

          
Which path?Most faculty use QTI import: it brings in every item, its key, and feedback at once. Pasting by hand works for a short quiz.

          

          
How to import a QTI file into Canvas
1. Paste the "QTI via text2qti" prompt after your final set. Save the AI's
   output as a plain .txt or .md file.
2. Convert it with text2qti (free, open source; ask your instructional
   technologist or run: pip install text2qti, then text2qti myquiz.md).
   This creates myquiz.zip.
3. In Canvas: Course → Settings → Import Course Content.
   Content type: QTI .zip file. Choose the .zip, then Import.
4. The quiz appears under Quizzes as an unpublished Classic Quiz.
   To use New Quizzes, migrate it from the quiz's menu.
5. Review every item in Canvas preview before publishing.
